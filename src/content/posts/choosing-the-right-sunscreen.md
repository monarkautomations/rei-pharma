The question we hear most in May is always the same: *“Do I really need SPF 50,
or is 30 enough?”* The answer depends on your skin and how long you stay
outside — but a few things hold for everyone.

## SPF measures time, not strength

SPF is not a "protection level" like hotel stars. It tells you how much longer
the sun takes to redden your skin compared to bare skin.

- **SPF 30** blocks about 97% of UVB rays.
- **SPF 50+** blocks about 98%.

The difference looks small, and it is. What matters far more is **how much**
you apply and **how often** you reapply, not the number on the bottle.

## Most people apply far too little

For face and neck you need roughly two fingers' worth — index and middle
finger, covered from base to tip. For an adult body, about 6–7 teaspoons. Apply
half the amount and you lose much more than half the protection.

## Reapply every two hours

Sunscreen wears off with sweat, water, and the rub of clothes or a towel.
Reapply:

- every two hours while you are outside,
- immediately after swimming or towelling dry,
- on cloudy days too — clouds let most UV through.

## Choose by skin type, not by price

- **Oily or acne-prone skin** — look for *non-comedogenic*, *oil-free*, gel or
  fluid textures.
- **Dry skin** — creams with glycerin or hyaluronic acid.
- **Sensitive skin or children** — mineral filters (zinc oxide, titanium
  dioxide) irritate less often.
- **After acid or retinol treatments** — SPF 50+, no exceptions.

## Do not forget the spots everyone forgets

Ears, the back of the neck, the tops of the feet, lips, and the hairline. For
lips use a balm with SPF, not your face cream.
