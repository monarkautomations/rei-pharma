Every autumn brings the same question at the counter: *“I'm losing a lot of
hair, what should I take?”* The honest answer is that often you should take
nothing.

## What is normal

A person loses on average **50 to 100 strands a day**. In late summer and
autumn that number rises for a few weeks, because a larger share of hairs
enters the resting phase at the same time. It usually settles by itself within
two or three months.

## When it is worth worrying

Go to a doctor, not to the pharmacy, if:

- **bald patches** appear with clear edges,
- the scalp is red, crusted or painful,
- shedding continues **beyond three or four months** without easing,
- it comes with heavy fatigue, weight change or changes to your cycle — this
  can be thyroid or iron deficiency.

The usual tests are iron with ferritin, thyroid function, and vitamin D.
Without knowing those, any supplement is guesswork.

## What actually helps meanwhile

- **Stop making it worse.** Tight ponytails, very hot hairdryers and combing
  wet hair break strands that were not going to fall on their own.
- **Wash as often as you need.** Washing less does not stop hair falling — it
  just all falls on the day you wash.
- **Care for the scalp**, not only the lengths. Anti-shedding shampoos work on
  the scalp; leave them on for a minute or two.
- **Protein and iron in your diet.** Hair is among the first things to suffer
  on a very restricted diet.

## About supplements

Supplements with biotin, zinc or iron help **only if you are deficient**. If
your levels are normal, there is no evidence they add hair — and iron taken
without need can do harm.
