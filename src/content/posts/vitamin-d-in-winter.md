Vitamin D does not come mainly from food — your body makes it when skin
receives UVB rays. The problem is that from late October until March the sun
sits too low for this, even on clear days.

## Who runs low most often

- People who work indoors all day.
- Older adults — skin produces it more slowly with age.
- Those who cover up or use sunscreen year round.
- People with darker skin, who need more exposure.

## The signs are vague, so do not guess

Tiredness, muscle aches, low mood, frequent illness — all of these have dozens
of other causes. **The only way to know is a blood test (25-OH vitamin D).** It
is inexpensive and it tells you the dose.

## How to take it

Vitamin D is fat-soluble. Take it **with your main meal**, not on an empty
stomach — absorption improves considerably. D3 (cholecalciferol) absorbs better
than D2.

The common forms:

- **Daily capsules or tablets** — the easiest to remember.
- **Drops** — suitable for children and for small doses.
- **Weekly or monthly doses** — only on a doctor's instruction.

## How much to take

That is not decided by an article. The dose depends on your blood level, age,
weight and other medicines. Vitamin D accumulates in the body, and very high
doses over a long period can do harm.

**Ask the pharmacist or your doctor before starting**, especially if you take
heart medicines, diuretics, or have kidney problems.
